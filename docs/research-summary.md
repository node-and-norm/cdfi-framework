Reviewed 29 October 2026. This account synthesizes the linked records at source revision `54d58532ada2cef692d088c3881a1a92f40f68cc`. It adds no validation claim, release decision or change to the underlying method.

## Question and contribution

Which failures should disqualify an otherwise persuasive answer, and who can justify and review that judgment?

A source-linked scoring architecture translated from seven AI safety publications for SAICRED. Scoring-contract reconciliation and human theological validation remain open.

## Findings to date

The work translates selected evaluation ideas into domain-specific risks, observable failure modes, scoring rules, cap gates and proposed judge-reliability checks. The source-to-method map preserves those design steps.

The website’s preserved inspection of CDFI snapshot 73a9767195a42d83af00eb9ae26755fe2db5e537 records differences in input scales and weights among the formula, configuration and calculator. These are dated snapshot observations; later commits do not automatically resolve them.

## How to use the work

Inspect and adapt evaluation requirements, scoring rules and review procedures with qualified domain oversight.

## Current status and why

The framework remains provisional. Its admission boundary retains scoring-contract reconciliation and human theological validation as open work. The framework, SAICRED’s production pipeline and the external project’s publication authority remain distinct.

## Limits and next evidence

Literature-informed design choices do not establish the validity of particular weights, thresholds or deployment tiers. Reported model rankings and automated-judge checks do not replace human theological validation.

Reconcile the governing scoring contract, reproduce results against its exact inputs and configuration, and obtain the required qualified human review before stronger publication or institutional-use claims.
